# Claude Design prompt — GSL Playoff Splash Screen

---

Design a full-screen loading animation for a soccer league's playoff season — the GTA Super League, or "GSL." It plays when the site opens, runs about 3 seconds, then clears to reveal the site.

The whole screen is red — the brand red `#EF4444`, deepening to a darker crimson at the edges so the center glows like stadium light. Everything drawn on top of it is white.

A playoff bracket builds itself across the screen. Every team is just a circle — no names, no logos, no text anywhere in it. Eight circles appear, four on the left and four on the right, in a classic mirrored bracket with thin connector lines running inward. They pop in fast, staggered, outermost first.

Then they advance. From each matchup one circle travels inward along its line to the next round, and the one it beat fades back to a faint ghost. Eight become four. Four become two. It should feel like a tournament resolving itself — quick, decisive, each arrival landing with a small flash.

The last two circles slide toward each other and meet dead center. On contact there's a bright bloom, and the surviving circle turns gold — `#FBBF24` — with a warm glow around it. That gold circle morphs into a trophy, and it happens fast.

The trophy then dissolves into the league's identity. The GSL logo sits at the top, a square badge with a soft white glow. Beneath it, "GTA SUPER LEAGUE" in wide-tracked uppercase. Beneath that, "PLAYOFFS" — the hero of the whole screen, big and heavy and spread wide, arriving with its letters expanding outward. It should carry more weight than anything else on screen.

A beat to hold, then the whole thing wipes away.

The feel is broadcast sports graphics — a playoff sting on TSN or Sky Sports. Sharp, confident, fast. Precise thin lines, decisive motion, hard cuts on the flashes. Everything snaps into place instead of drifting there. Nothing bouncy, no glassmorphism, no soft gradients on the text. A red-and-gold broadcast package, not a web template.

It has to look as good on a phone as on a desktop — the mirrored bracket should scale down cleanly rather than crowd the edges.
